Why the Stock Fell 34% While the Business Kept Compounding

The disconnect breaks down into a few layers:

Layer 1 — It was priced at a premium multiple heading into 2025. ISRG has long traded at one of the richest valuations in medtech, reflecting its dominant, decades-long moat in robotic-assisted surgery. At $610, the stock was priced for flawless execution. Any deceleration — even a modest one — was going to compress that multiple hard.

Layer 2 — Tariff headwinds. Management has explicitly guided for an estimated tariff impact of about 1% of revenue on 2026 gross margin. That's a real, quantified cost pressure tied to trade policy, not a demand problem — but the market has been punishing any medtech name with tariff exposure regardless of how well the underlying business is absorbing it.

Layer 3 — Sector-wide medtech de-rating. ISRG's decline has tracked a broader premium-medtech selloff in 2026, not an ISRG-specific breakdown. The stock's ~29% six-month decline sits alongside similar pressure across the sector as investors rotate away from richly-valued healthcare names generally.

Layer 4 — A high bar to clear. When a stock has compounded as consistently as ISRG has over two decades, every quarter gets judged against an extremely high bar. A 17% procedure growth quarter that would be a career-defining result for most medtech companies reads as merely "in line" against ISRG's own history — and "in line" isn't enough to defend a premium multiple in a risk-off tape.
