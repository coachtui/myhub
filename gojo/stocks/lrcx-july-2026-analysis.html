What Lam Research Actually Does

Lam Research (NASDAQ: LRCX) makes the equipment used to manufacture semiconductors — specifically etch systems (which carve circuit patterns into wafers), deposition systems (which deposit thin films layer by layer), and cleaning systems. Every major fab on the planet — TSMC, Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron, Intel — depends on Lam equipment to build chips. You cannot make advanced NAND, DRAM, or leading-edge logic at scale without it.

This matters for two reasons right now: first, AI data centers require enormous quantities of HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) and advanced DRAM, driving memory fab expansion. Second, the NAND market — Lam's largest end market — is recovering from a brutal 2023–2024 oversupply cycle. Both tailwinds are hitting simultaneously, and the numbers show it.

Two consecutive records. The NAND recovery is real, the HBM demand is real, and the CHIPS Act domestic fab buildout (TSMC Arizona, Samsung Texas, Intel Ohio) is generating incremental equipment spend on top of existing upgrade cycles. Lam is getting paid from all three pools at once.

A 34% operating margin on a business making physical equipment is exceptional. For comparison, most industrial manufacturers run 10–15%. Lam's margins reflect deep customer lock-in — once a fab is built around Lam etch tools, switching costs are enormous. You don't rip out $500M in equipment mid-production run to try a competitor.

The $6B in TTM free cash flow is the number that matters most. That's the engine funding the dividend, the buybacks, and the R&D that maintains Lam's technology edge. FCF grew 58% year-over-year — the profitability inflection is accelerating, not just steady.

Key Risks

  • NAND cycle timing: If memory manufacturers pull back capex faster than expected, equipment orders slow. Lam has less control over customer timing than over its own execution.
