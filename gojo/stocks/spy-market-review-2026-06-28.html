VIX — The Fear Gauge

VIX eased back over the course of the week after an earlier wobble, consistent with a market that got nervous mid-week and calmed down into Friday’s close — the opposite trajectory of a building panic. That matters: a falling VIX into a week that closed lower on price is a real divergence worth watching, not a confirmation of stress. By Monday June 29, with SPY rallying back toward $742, VIX had fallen further still. The pattern this week looks more like a sentiment overshoot that volatility markets didn’t fully buy into, rather than the start of a genuine de-risking event.

Directional Thesis

Bias: NEUTRAL — Sentiment Overshot, Technicals Didn’t Confirm.

Four signals need to be weighed honestly this week:

1. The technical structure never broke. SPY stayed roughly 6% above its 200-day MA all week and only tested, not broke, its 50-day. This was a sentiment-driven pullback, not a structural trend change. Don’t let the Extreme Fear headline imply more technical damage than actually occurred.

2. VIX declining into a down week is the key divergence. If this were a real de-risking event, VIX would have risen alongside the Fear & Greed drop. It didn’t. Options markets are not confirming the level of stress retail sentiment readings suggest.

3. F&G at 25 with price still near highs has historically been a better setup for a bounce than for further declines. Extreme Fear without proportional price damage is a classic capitulation-without-confirmation pattern.

4. The macro backdrop (sticky PCE, hawkish Fed, AI cost pass-through) is unresolved, not improving. This caps how bullish the read can be — the structural risks from the June 17 FOMC are all still on the table heading into Friday’s jobs report.
