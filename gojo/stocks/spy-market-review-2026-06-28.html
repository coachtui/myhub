What Drove the Week: The AI Hardware Bill Arrives

The trigger for this week’s selloff was not a macro surprise — it was Apple. The company announced price hikes of up to 20% on MacBook and iPad lineups in key markets, citing surging input costs for high-bandwidth memory chips. Apple shares fell 6.1%, wiping out $250–263 billion in market cap. Microsoft followed with Xbox console price increases, dragging its stock down 4.0%. The message was blunt: the AI infrastructure buildout is expensive, and those costs are now flowing downstream into consumer products. Investors who had priced in infinite AI margin expansion suddenly had to grapple with demand elasticity. Alphabet, Meta, and Nvidia fell 0.5%–3.5% over the week as the market repriced the entire hyperscaler capex story. This wasn’t a sector rotation — it was a structural valuation correction on everything priced for AI exceptionalism.

The global contagion started Monday when South Korea’s Kospi, which had briefly touched an all-time high of 9,000 just a week earlier, reversed violently. The index triggered mandatory circuit breakers twice during the week — once for the full exchange — and closed Friday down 5.81% to 8,411. SK Hynix fell 8.4% and Samsung fell 5.3% as institutional money unwound crowded HBM (high-bandwidth memory) positions. The Nikkei fell 4.15% on Friday alone, with SoftBank plunging nearly 13% on reports that OpenAI may delay its IPO to 2027. The spillover into US markets on Tuesday extended the two-day tech rout that headlines the week.

The Divergence: Hardware Wins, Software Pays

Not everything sold off. The divergence this week was sharp and meaningful. Micron Technology surged 15.7% after reporting blowout earnings and issuing a record $50 billion revenue guide, confirming that upstream chip suppliers retain strong pricing power even as downstream consumers absorb the cost shock. SanDisk gained 22%. Western Digital, Seagate, and Qualcomm all moved higher. The Dow Jones — weighted toward financials, industrials, and healthcare rather than mega-cap tech — gained 0.60% for the week. Defensive sectors outperformed broadly. Healthcare, utilities, and consumer staples all held. This is a classic late-cycle rotation signal: money moving from high-multiple growth into value and yield. Whether it lasts or reverses sharply next week is the key question for June 30–July 4.

The Macro Picture: PCE Above 4%, Rate Hike Probability at 80%

May PCE inflation — the Fed’s preferred measure — came in at 4.1% year-over-year, the first print above 4% in three years. Core PCE (ex food and energy) rose 0.3% month-over-month and 3.4% annually. Q1 GDP was revised up to 2.1% and initial jobless claims fell 12,000 to 215,000, painting a picture of a resilient economy that the Fed has less cover to cut into. Markets are now pricing an 80% probability of at least one 25-basis-point rate hike before year-end. The one disinflationary bright spot: Brent crude slipped below $73 after the US and Iran signed an interim memorandum on shipping access through the Strait of Hormuz, reducing the Middle East supply premium that had pushed PCE higher in the first place. Energy easing helps on the headline print but doesn’t move the core number enough to change the Fed’s posture.
