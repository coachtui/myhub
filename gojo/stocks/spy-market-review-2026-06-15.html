SPY Weekly: CPI 4.2%, VIX at 19 — FOMC Week Demands Caution

SPY retreats to $742 from its $759.57 all-time high as May inflation reaccelerates; Kevin Warsh's debut SEP and dot plot on June 17 are the week's defining catalyst.

Price Action and Technical Structure

SPY closed Thursday June 12 at $742.36, roughly 2.3% below its June 2 all-time closing high of $759.57, as risk-off sentiment swept back into markets on rising bond yields (10-year at 4.47–4.53%) and a hotter-than-expected May CPI print. The week's low of $734.54 (June 11) held as an intraday support test, and SPY recovered into the close — but the ATH rejection at $760 is technically meaningful. The 14-day RSI at ~67.5 remains elevated (overbought territory begins at 70), and the MACD is bullish on the short-term timeframe but showing a sell signal on the 3-month MACD, flagging decelerating momentum. Both moving averages remain firmly below price, keeping the macro uptrend intact despite the near-term air pocket.

Macro Snapshot

The macro story this week is uncomfortable: the US is running 4.2% headline CPI on the back of an energy shock driven by the Iran conflict (energy prices +23.5% YoY), while the labor market holds firm at 4.3% unemployment and GDP growth came in at +1.6–2.0% annualized for Q1 2026. The Fed is caught in a box — inflation well above target, but growth not strong enough to warrant aggressive tightening. New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh inherits the mess at the June 16-17 FOMC, where 99.5% of market participants expect no rate change. The real read is in Wednesday's dot plot and press conference.

VIX — The Fear Gauge

The VIX closed at 19.25 on June 12 (down slightly from 19.44 on June 11), hovering just below the psychologically important 20 threshold. This is not calm — the VIX spent most of May in the 15–17 range before the CPI shock and bond yield spike this week pushed it back toward the 20 line. For context, the VIX briefly surged above 30 in mid-March as Iran tensions first escalated and oil prices spiked. We've recovered from that panic, but we haven't returned to complacency either. A VIX closing above 20 on FOMC week would be a genuine warning shot for the bulls.

  • Below 15 Calm — Low Volatility / Complacency
  • 15–20 Normal — Moderate Caution
  • 19.25 ← WE ARE HERE Elevated — Approaching Warning Zone
  • Above 30 Crisis — Panic / Extreme Fear (March 2026 spike)
⚠ VIX Watch: At 19.25, the VIX is within 0.75 points of the 20 threshold — the line that historically marks the shift from "elevated caution" to "institutional hedging mode." With FOMC on Tuesday-Wednesday and bond yields at 16-month highs (4.53%), any hawkish signal from Warsh could push VIX through 20 and trigger forced selling. Monitor closely.

Fear & Greed Index — Sentiment Read

The CNN Fear & Greed Index closed the week at 34 — Fear, a sharp reversal from the Greed territory (~65) the index occupied in April when the market was pricing in Fed cuts and AI momentum was unchallenged. The reacceleration of CPI, the Iran conflict's energy shock, and this week's bond yield spike have collectively drained bullish sentiment. At 34, the index is pricing genuine fear — but not yet Extreme Fear (below 25). The composite reading is consistent with a market that is worried but not panicking.

Recession probability remains contained. Yield-curve models put recession risk below 15% over the next 12 months, and the Sahm Rule indicator at 0.10 is far below its 0.50 recession trigger. Polymarket's market-implied odds price an 84.5% chance of no recession by end-2026. However, if CPI continues accelerating past 4.5% and the Fed is forced to hike into slowing growth, that recession probability could reprice quickly — watch the July CPI release (July 14) as the next major data checkpoint.

Directional Thesis

Bias: NEUTRAL with bearish tilt — "Wait for FOMC Resolution"

This is not a week to be a hero in either direction. Here is the full read:

  1. Technicals are bullish but stalling. SPY is above its 50-day ($711.90) and 200-day ($683.44) MAs — the uptrend is intact. But the ATH rejection at $760, the 3-month MACD sell signal, and RSI near 70 all say the easy money from the April–June run has already been made. The next 3–5% is earned, not given.
  2. Macro is the headwind. Three consecutive months of CPI acceleration (4.2% in May), energy up 23.5% driven by the Iran conflict, and 10-year yields hitting 4.53% create a stagflationary overhang. The equity risk premium is thin. Bonds at 4.5%+ are a real competitor to equities for the first time since 2023.
  3. VIX and F&G say "handle with care." VIX at 19.25 and Fear & Greed at 34 together signal institutional participants are hedging, not buying the dip aggressively. Five of seven F&G sub-indexes are in Fear. This is not contrarian buy-the-fear territory — sentiment is not Extreme Fear (sub-20). It's the uncomfortable middle ground where you wait for a catalyst to resolve direction.
  4. FOMC is the week's fulcrum. Kevin Warsh's June 17 press conference and the Summary of Economic Projections are the most important events of the coming week — potentially the most important FOMC event of 2026. If the dot plot signals rate hikes later this year, expect SPY to retest $730 support immediately. If Warsh signals patience and pivot readiness for H2 2026, the $760 ATH gets retested by month-end.
