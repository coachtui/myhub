The Numbers (Q1 2026)

  • Revenue: $56.31B — +33% YoY, beat $55.45B estimate
  • EPS: $10.44 reported — beat $6.72 estimate; $8.03B tax benefit inflates this — adjusted EPS ~$7.31
  • Net Income: $26.8B — up from $16.6B prior year
  • Operating Margin: 41%
  • Daily Active People (DAP): 3.56B — +4% YoY, missed 3.62B estimate; -5% QoQ
  • Q2 Revenue Guidance: $58B–$61B (analysts expected $59.5B — in line)
  • Capex Guidance: Raised to $125B–$145B (up from $115B–$135B)

What the Numbers Actually Say

Strip the $8.03B tax benefit out of the headline EPS and you get ~$7.31 — still a strong beat over the $6.72 estimate, but the $10.44 headline will mislead. The operating story is what matters: 41% operating margin on $56B in revenue is exceptional for a consumer internet business at this scale. Meta is running the most profitable advertising machine ever built.

Revenue at +33% YoY on a $56B base is remarkable. The ad pricing and inventory optimization from AI-driven targeting is the engine. Advertisers who cut budgets in 2023 have come back and they're spending more per impression because Meta's AI is delivering better return on ad spend than any alternative.

The DAP Miss — How Much to Care

3.56B daily active people against an expectation of 3.62B is a miss. The -5% QoQ decline is likely seasonal (Q4 always peaks with holiday engagement) but the YoY growth of only 4% signals Meta is approaching saturation in developed markets. The growth story is now primarily monetization — getting more revenue per user rather than more users.

That's not a bad story. It's just a different story than 2019. At 41% margins and $56B quarterly revenue, Meta doesn't need user growth — it needs ad pricing to hold. So far, it is.

The Capex Raise — Zuckerberg is Betting Big

Raising the annual capex ceiling to $145B signals Zuckerberg's conviction that AI infrastructure spend today = competitive moat in 3–5 years. The risk is that $145B in capex on a $224B annualized revenue base is aggressive. FCF will compress. The bull case is that Meta's AI investments (Llama, AI assistants, ad optimization) extend the monetization advantage for another decade. The bear case is that it's capital destruction chasing a capability that open-source alternatives will commoditize.

Zuckerberg has been right on this bet before (Instagram, WhatsApp, doubling down on Reels). He's also been catastrophically wrong (metaverse). This capex raise requires monitoring.

Trade Evaluation

Setup: 41% operating margins, 33% revenue growth, dominant ad platform with no credible near-term challenger, $145B AI infrastructure bet that has historically paid off.

Risk: DAP saturation limits user-growth-driven revenue tailwinds. Capex $145B ceiling creates FCF compression through 2027. Regulatory risk in EU and US (antitrust scrutiny of Instagram/WhatsApp). AI advertising advantage could be eroded if Google or TikTok Shop out-innovates on targeting.

After This Print: Revenue beat and operating margin at 41% should drive a positive reaction. DAP miss and capex raise will generate noise. Net-net this is a strong quarter. Any post-earnings dip on capex concern is the setup — the ad revenue engine is structurally intact.

Catalyst to Watch: Meta AI assistant adoption metrics and whether Llama-based products start generating revenue contribution in H2 2026. If AI becomes a direct revenue line (not just ad optimization backend), the multiple re-rates significantly higher.

Bottom Line

Revenue +33%, operating margin 41%, and ad pricing holding — Meta's core business is performing at a level most companies can't touch. The DAP miss is a rounding error on a 3.56B user base. The capex raise is the debate — and it should be watched carefully. But at 41% margins and growing revenue 33% YoY, this is not a company in trouble. It's a cash machine that's reinvesting aggressively in its own future.
