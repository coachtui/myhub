Macro Snapshot

The hard data is unchanged from last week except for inflation, which re-accelerated. September CPI (released Wednesday) came in at 3.7% headline (3.4% in August; +0.6% m/m, mostly gasoline) and 2.6% core (2.4% prior, +0.3% m/m versus +0.2% expected). The Fed raised rates 25bp to 3.75%–4.00% on September 16 in a unanimous vote, and Chair Warsh’s comments at the IMF meetings pulled October hike odds back to about 13–15% after they briefly jumped to 41%. August PCE was 3.4% headline and 3.0% core, Q2 GDP is 2.2%, and September unemployment is 4.2% after a payroll gain of only 29,000.

Rates helped stocks less than they should have. The 10-year touched 5.33% after the CPI print but fell about 15bp on the week to roughly 5.1% (its best week since February) as money fled equities for bonds. Oil moved the other way: Brent settled at $109.86 and WTI at $101.41 on U.S.–Iran, Houthi and Russia–Ukraine tensions. Falling yields plus rising oil is a stagflation-flavored mix, not a clean risk-on one.
