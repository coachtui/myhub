The Business

Marvell (NASDAQ: MRVL) makes the data-infrastructure silicon that sits between the AI chips and the network. Two things matter now, and both point at the AI data center:

  • Custom AI silicon (XPUs). Marvell co-designs bespoke accelerators and compute chips for hyperscalers — the "not-Nvidia" chips that Amazon, Microsoft, Google and others build in-house with a silicon partner. This scaled from zero to ~$1.5B in a few years, and management says it accelerates in the back half of this year. They cite 18 multi-generational sockets won and 50+ pipeline opportunities worth $75B+ in lifetime revenue, targeting ~20% of a ~$55B custom-silicon market by 2028.
  • Optical + interconnect. High-speed optical DSPs, 800G and 1.6T optics, 51.2T Ethernet switches, and datacenter-interconnect modules — the plumbing that moves data between AI servers. This is the same optical-interconnect wave the rest of the AI-infra complex is riding.

There's still a legacy enterprise-networking and carrier business, but the story — and the re-rating — is Marvell shifting from a diversified chip supplier into an AI-infrastructure enabler. The one framing to keep in mind: in both of its growth engines, Marvell is the strong number two to Broadcom. That's the whole bull-and-bear tension in a sentence.

What Q2 Actually Said

The quarter itself was genuinely strong — this is not a stumbling company:

  • Revenue: $2.739B, a record, up 37% year over year and ahead of the ~$2.70B guide
  • Data center: revenue growth accelerated to +46% YoY — the number that matters most, and it's speeding up, not slowing
  • EPS (non-GAAP): $0.94, a hair above the $0.93 guide midpoint — a beat, but a modest one
  • Margins: 53.1% GAAP gross margin, ~59% non-GAAP — healthy and expanding
  • Guidance: Q3 revenue guided to ~$3.0B (±5%), a big sequential step and ~40% YoY. Critically, management raised the revenue outlook for both FY2027 and FY2028 again, citing "exceptionally robust" AI bookings
  • The second engine: the custom business is called out to significantly accelerate in the back half of FY2027 — that's the swing factor for the whole thesis, and there's an Investor Day on October 6 to detail it

Read plainly: growth is accelerating, the outlook went up (twice now), and margins are expanding. On the fundamentals, this was a good quarter.

Why It Fell on a Beat

So why did the stock drop? Same reason NVDA slipped on its own beat this week, and the same reason the whole AI-infrastructure complex keeps selling good news: the bar is now set at perfection.

The multiple ran ahead of even great results. After a 125% move, MRVL trades around 60x forward earnings and high-teens times sales. At that price, a modest beat — EPS a penny over guide — isn't a catalyst; it's a disappointment relative to what's priced in. The market wanted a blowout and got merely excellent.

Concentration is the recurring fear. The custom-silicon business leans on a handful of hyperscalers, with heavy exposure to a couple of big accounts. Investors remember December 2025, when rumors that Microsoft might use Broadcom for custom chips knocked the stock down double digits in days. Every quarter, the tape re-prices the risk that a hyperscaler in-houses a future chip generation or shifts to the bigger rival.

AI-capex digestion. The macro overhang pressuring the entire group — the worry that hyperscaler spending is near a peak rate of change — hits the highest-multiple names hardest, and MRVL is one of them.

The Thesis

Bull case: the growth is accelerating, not fading — data center +46% and speeding up, the outlook raised twice, margins expanding, and a second engine (custom silicon) inflecting in the back half with 18 sockets and a $75B pipeline behind it. Add optical/interconnect leadership into the 1.6T cycle and the Celestial AI photonics acquisition for optionality, and you have a company genuinely re-rating from "diversified semi" to "core AI-infra." If the custom ramp lands, this quarter's numbers are a floor, not a peak, and the October 6 Investor Day is the next catalyst to prove it.

Bear case: ~60x forward earnings prices in years of flawless execution, and the sell-on-a-beat is the market telling you the bar is impossibly high. Strip the acceleration and you're paying a premium multiple for the number two player in both custom silicon and optical DSPs — behind a much larger Broadcom that can press on price and design wins. The demand is real but concentrated; one lost program or one hyperscaler going in-house resets the story fast.

What would change my mind: a custom-program delay or loss — a hyperscaler in-sourcing a generation — is the thesis-breaker. So is meaningful optical-DSP share loss to Broadcom, or data-center growth guidance decelerating back below ~40%. Any of those turns this from "expensive but accelerating" into "expensive and slowing," which is a very different stock.
