The Business

Super Micro (NASDAQ: SMCI) builds servers — increasingly, whole AI data-center racks. Historically it was a low-margin box builder: take Nvidia GPUs and Intel/AMD silicon, assemble optimized systems, ship them fast, earn single-digit gross margins on enormous volume. The bull thesis for the last two years has been that the AI buildout turns SMCI's speed-to-market and liquid-cooling expertise into something more valuable than assembly. The company now brands itself around "Data Center Building Block Solutions" (DCBBS) — selling design, integration, and full-rack deployment rather than boxes. That is the transformation this quarter is supposed to prove.

The baggage is real: an accounting scandal that saw auditor Ernst & Young resign in 2024, a delayed 10-K, a near-delisting, a 10-for-1 split, and an independent board review tied to export-control matters. Every number below is company-reported and unaudited. That context is not a footnote here — it is central to how much weight the print deserves.

What Q4 Actually Said

Fiscal Q4 2026 (quarter ended June 30, 2026), reported after the close on August 11:

  • Revenue: $11.1B, up 93% year over year and 9% sequentially — near the high end of the $11.0–12.5B guide, roughly in line with consensus
  • GAAP gross margin: 17.5% — versus 9.9% in Q3 and 9.5% a year ago. This is the headline.
  • GAAP diluted EPS: $1.62, versus $0.72 last quarter and $0.31 a year ago
  • Non-GAAP diluted EPS: $1.70, against a guide of $0.65–0.79 — more than double the top end
  • Net income: $1.18B, versus $483M in Q3 and $195M a year ago
  • Operating cash flow: +$747M in the quarter against just $25M of capex — genuinely free-cash-flow positive for the period
  • Full year FY2026: revenue ~$39.1B (up 78%), non-GAAP diluted EPS ~$3.63, over $60B in new orders booked, record backlog into FY2027
  • FY2027 guidance: revenue guided as high as $72B — nearly double fiscal 2026

Strip the noise and there are two stories. The first is revenue: fine, in line, up huge year over year — not the surprise. The second is margin, and it is the entire move. A hardware assembler does not casually go from 6.3% gross margin two quarters ago to 9.9% to 17.5% now. That is a near-tripling in six months, and it is the difference between SMCI being a thin-margin commodity shop and being a real infrastructure business.

Do You Believe the Margin?

This is the whole take, so let me split it honestly.

The bull reading: the mix shifted. Management credits a richer enterprise customer base and broad DCBBS adoption — full-rack, liquid-cooled, design-integrated deployments carry structurally higher margins than shipping bare GPU servers to hyperscalers. If that is what happened, 17.5% is not a spike, it is a new baseline, and the earnings power at $50–72B of revenue on mid-teens margins is enormous against a company that closed today worth roughly $22B. On ~$3.63 of trailing non-GAAP EPS, the stock trades under 9x earnings. If FY2027 revenue and margin are anywhere near the guide, the forward multiple is low-to-mid single digits. That is a value case hiding inside an AI momentum name.

The skeptic's reading: the numbers are unaudited, this is the company whose auditor walked, and a gross margin that nearly doubles in one quarter is exactly the kind of figure you wait for the 10-K to confirm. Margins can jump for reasons that do not repeat — favorable component pricing, a rich-mix quarter that normalizes, inventory and cost-timing effects. And the balance sheet is doing something loud: inventories ballooned to $12.9B from $4.7B a year ago, accounts receivable to $6.1B from $2.2B. The company funded that working-capital surge with a new ~$4.2B mandatory convertible preferred raise and roughly $4B of term loans and credit lines on top of $4.7B of convertible notes. A single strong operating-cash-flow quarter (+$747M) does not erase a year of aggressive balance-sheet expansion to chase the AI ramp.

My read: the quarter is genuinely good and the market is right to re-rate it — but the margin is a claim, not yet a fact, and the inventory build is the tell that this growth is being bought, not just earned. The bull case needs 17% to hold for a second quarter and needs the audit to bless it. Until then it is a high-conviction bet on management credibility at a company that has spent the credibility before.
